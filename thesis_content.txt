Chapter 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1             Background of the Study
In today’s businesses, competition lies in making informed decisions to maximize operational efficiency. Modern Information Technology Service Management (ITSM) environments continue to grow in scale and complexity, requiring proactive solutions for resource planning, performance monitoring, and early risk detection to meet Service-Level Agreements (SLAs). This project is commissioned by a professional from Cognizant, a leading global provider of IT help desk support, who recognized the need for a more intelligent decision-support framework for their team. While the system is being developed to address the operational challenges identified by this client, Cognizant as an organization may also benefit should the solution be proposed to its management, given the company’s high-volume support operations and reliance on ITSM processes.
Decision-support frameworks backed with analytics are becoming more and more prevalent in the ITSM field. By predicting ticket loads, optimizing staffing, identifying inefficiencies across assignment groups, and spotting possible service problems before they worsen, research indicates that machine learning-based prediction models would significantly increase operational efficiency (Stein et al., 2018; Al-Hawari & Barham, 2019; Nabovati et al., 2023). Additionally, it has been demonstrated that intelligent dashboards and 
diagnostic tools improve management visibility, simplify workload distribution, 
and lessen operational bottlenecks (Hidayat et al., 2024; Jevsejev et al., 2018; Avancha et al., 2024) which could contribute the current state of Cognizant’s operational level where key processes have yet to be properly automated for 
ease of life. The project focuses on the creation of “Work-Wise”, an integrated multi-level decision support system that combines early event detection, performance diagnostics, and predictive forecasting into a single web-based platform. To improve work-efficiency at Cognizant’s current state, Work-Wise will utilize weekly loaded data to predict ticket volumes, use Key Performance Indicators (KPI) to grade team performance, identify high-risk non First Call Resolution (FCR) problems, and suggest the best staffing and workload distribution plans. This cohesive strategy is consistent with studies that highlight the value of data-driven workload management and predictive intelligence in enhancing operational resilience and the quality of IT services (Schwarz, 2015; Galanti et al., 2022; Angula et al., 2025).
The shift to digitalization, automation, and proactivity in modern work calls for a need for intelligent and proactive IT support solutions that can guarantee effective service delivery and performance stability. One of the top firms in the world of digital transformation, Cognizant oversees a variety of high-volume support operations that significantly depend on ITSM procedures to ensure constant SLA compliance and client happiness (Cognizant, n.d.). The operational burden of non-FCR events, unequal performance between assignment groups, and variable ticket workloads are three enduring difficulties that many businesses still face despite developments in ITSM standards.
Historically, IT support teams used traditional manual procedures or the judgment of their managers to decide how many people to hire, which often led to imprecise management of workload to staffing ratio. In research, studies highlight 
how predictive analytics anticipate changes in demand and make better use of their human resources (CIO Influence, 2025; Xue, 2018). When compared to conventional techniques, forecasting models like those studied by Stein et al. (2018) show noticeably higher accuracy, highlighting the necessity of machine learning-driven workforce intelligence in IT service environments. Long resolution times, variable service quality, and SLA violations are all caused by performance variability among assignment groups. Managers are unable to determine which teams need more training, performance support, or workload reassignment in the absence of defined KPIs (Ghani, 2021; Nabovati et al., 2023). While studies by Jevsejev et al. (2018) and Avancha et al. (2024) demonstrate how Machine Learning-enchanced (ML) dashboards improve operational decision-making, research by Hidayat et al. (2024) demonstrates how machine learning may support automated task balancing. These results demonstrate the significance of intelligent load balancing and diagnostic analytics as essential features of a contemporary ITSM decision support system. These problems are made worse by non-FCR incidents, which have a significant detrimental impact on operational effectiveness, increase costs, and reduce customer satisfaction. High-risk service interactions have been shown to be accurately predicted by machine learning models, which enables more strategic resource allocation and early intervention (Ali et al., 2023; Omoniyi, 2024; Vyas & Vyas, 2022). Complementary research (Galanti et al., 2022; Shrestha et al., 2016; Angula et al., 2025) demonstrate the significance of predictive intelligence and Decision-Support Systems (DSS) in improving ITSM accuracy and encouraging continuous service improvement throughout digital operations.When combined, these elements provide a logical justification for creating Work-Wise wherein it tackles interrelated operational issues in ITSM by combining early non-FCR detection, diagnostic workload balance, and predictive forecasting into a single intelligent system providing the analytics to inform and enable Cognizant to make the most effective and rational decision. Organizations can handle complex and dynamic support environments more accurately and efficiently thanks to this unified platform, which guarantees proactive planning, optimal resource use, and enhanced service quality.
1.2    Objectives of the Study
General Objective
To develop Work-Wise, an intelligent multi-level decision support system based on machine learning to monitor operational health and performance, providing advisory of staff expansion, team performance recalibration, FCR adherence, and balancing of tasks among the IT support desk teams.
Specific Objectives
To create a multi-level decision-support system by integrating the predictive and diagnostic analytics into the web-based dashboard that monitors and provides. actionable insight based on operational health and performance through collated 
analytics of assignment group performance, resolution time, SLA compliance, FCR rate, and reopen rate for the ITSM team management of the client.
To balance ticket workload distribution ratio between ITSM Support Employees by applying diagnostic analytics to detect inefficiencies in ticket distribution that affect ITSM Support Employee KPIs (e.g. SLA Compliance, Resolution Time), and prescriptive analytics to recommend workload distribution that maximizes employee performance while maintaining a threshold ratio of ticket workload distribution.
To improve FCR by detecting Non-FCR risks through diagnostic analytics to identify drivers of Non-FCR such as ticket category and assigned employee, and prescriptive analytics that provide targeted intervention (e.g. training, reassignment, escalation) 
To recommend staffing needs based on predictive analytics of ITSM Support Employee KPIs (e.g. SLA Compliance, FCR Rate, Average Resolution Time) against forecasted seasonal ticket volume. Generating staffing recommendations such as hiring and staff distribution during high ticket volume periods.

1.4	Significance of the Study
Integrated Predictive, Diagnostic and Prescriptive DSS
The study will demonstrate the value of a unified decision‑support system that will integrate predictive analytics, diagnostic tools, and prescriptive recommendations into a single web‑based platform. By consolidating these analytical functions, the system will reduce SLA breaches, maximize utilization of operational hours, and improve resolution times for both FCR and non‑FCR tickets. The integration will ensure that operational inefficiencies will not only be detected but will also be addressed with actionable interventions, fostering a proactive workflow demanded by modern ITSM environments.I
ITSM‑Specific Staffing Intelligence
	The system will provide staffing recommendations tailored to ITSM operations, addressing recurring issues such as bottlenecks in task delegation and sub‑optimal staff allocation. Through predictive analytics, the DSS will forecast ticket volume against staff capacity and recommend actions such as hiring, 
redistribution, or reassignment. This capability will ensure that ITSM Support Employees will maintain balanced workloads and consistent KPIs, particularly during forecasted high‑traffic periods, thereby sustaining service quality and operational efficiency.
Explainable Dashboard Analytics
The web application will enhance visibility of KPIs through role‑based dashboards that will present data in an accessible and actionable format. Admins will gain oversight of user records and system access, managers will monitor tickets and workload balancing, employees will view their assigned tasks and performance metrics, and senior management/HR will receive aggregated insights and forecasts. These dashboards will make inefficiencies easy to identify, flag non‑FCR tickets for targeted resource allocation, and provide transparency in decision‑making, ensuring that analytics will be explainable and usable across organizational levels.
Hybrid KPI‑Based Machine Learning Framework
At the core of the system will be a hybrid machine learning framework that will leverage KPIs to drive workload balancing, FCR improvement, and staffing decisions. Models such as decision trees, clustering, regression, and time‑series forecasting will be applied to ticket and performance data, producing insights that will be both predictive and prescriptive. This hybrid approach will ensure that recommendations will be grounded in measurable performance indicators, aligning machine learning outputs with operational realities and enabling 
data‑driven improvements in ITSM processes.

1.5  Scope and Delimitations
Scope
		This study covers the design and development of Work‑Wise, a decision‑support system that supports managing operations in an ITSM environment. The system features analytical functions that process data in run‑time with ingestion through machine learning, including workload distribution that maximizes employee performance while maintaining a threshold ticket workload ratio among ITSM Support Employees. To achieve this, models such as Decision Trees and Random Forests classify workload distribution as balanced or imbalanced. The system also identifies Non‑FCR drivers such as ticket category and assigned employee, with targeted interventions (e.g., training, reassignment, escalation) supported by diagnostic and prescriptive analytics. This is enabled by Logistic Regression to predict first‑contact resolution probability. Furthermore, staffing recommendations such as hiring and staff redistribution are guided by predictive and prescriptive analytics, using Time Series Forecasting models (ARIMA) to anticipate ticket volume, Regression models to estimate SLA compliance and resolution times, and Simulation models to test staffing scenarios. 
Moreover, the project is commissioned by a professional from Cognizant, who identified the need for a more intelligent framework to improve team efficiency and workload management. While the system is tailored to address the commissioner’s operational challenges, it also demonstrates potential value for Cognizant as a whole, should the commissioner propose its adoption on a larger scale.
Limitations
		The system is limited to simulation in a controlled environment of manual ingestion of generated data during the development process and would only reach field-simulation until client user testing. The client commissioner can only provide sample data fields and values. As a result, while the project environment is equipped with proper data access and web hosting to enable predictive, diagnostic, and prescriptive analytics– the prototype operates in a controlled setting, disabling the system’s dynamic data updating or live monitoring until given access to a continuous feed of data. In addition, the system is confined to the commissioner’s team, unless they choose to propose its adoption more broadly.
1.6. Definition of Terms
		This part of the paper presents the operational and technical terms used in this study. Each term is clearly defined based on how it is used within the context of the research to avoid ambiguity and to help readers better understand the concepts, variables, and processes discussed in the study.
Operational Terms
		Work‑Wise System. Refers to the multi-level intelligent decision support system that was developed in this study. It supports workload management and operational planning in the ITSM systems through the use of automated recommendations, performance diagnostics, and predictive forecasting.
		First Call Resolution (FCR). This refers to a support ticket that has been solved successfully on the caller’s first contact with an IT support agent. The non–FCR, also known as the failure of FCR serves as an early warning for potentially serious service encounters in this study. FCR is utilized as a measure of customer satisfaction and efficiency of service.
		Service Level Agreement (SLA). It indicates the predetermined performance goals and service requirements that an IT support team must meet, especially with regard to the response and resolution times. SLA compliance is addressed in this study as a crucial operational parameter for determining whether assignment groups continue to provide satisfactory service.
		Information Technology Service Management (ITSM). Is referring to the organized collection of the best practices, principles, and procedures used in the creation, management and enhancement of IT services. ITSM serves as the operational environment for Work-Wise in this, allowing for forecasting, workload optimization, and performance monitoring.
		Information Technology (IT). Pertains to the technical resources, infrastructures, and systems utilized for information processing, storage, and management. In this study, it represents the domain that generates performance statistics and service tickets, which the Work-Wise system analyzes for decision support.
		Ticket Data. Defines the organized collection of data that is recorded for 
every request for IT support request. The core datasets for forecasting, diagnostics, and performance evaluations in this study comprises ticket data, which contains fields like priority, timestamps, category, assignment group, SLA status, FCR status, and resolution duration.
		Machine Learning (ML). This refers to the computational methods that the systems use to generate predictions or classifications by acquiring knowledge from previous ticket data. In this study, ML models enable the Work-Wise platform’s automated recommendation engine, forecast workloads, and identify non-FCR threats.
		Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). Signify measurable metrics that are used to assess how well assignment groups and overall service operations are performing. KPIs including SLA compliance, median resolution time, FCR rate, and ticket reopen rate are employed in this study for dashboard and diagnostic reporting analysis.
		Decision Support System (DSS). pertains to the integrated platform that offers analytics, forecasts, diagnostics and automated suggestions to assist IT managers make well-informed decisions. Work-Wise functions as DSS in this study, incorporating workload analysis, KPI tracking and machine learning outputs into a unified web-based environment.
		Bottleneck. It refers to a stage in the IT support workflow where high workload on a particular assignment group or procedure, unequal task distribution, or restricted capacity cause the flow of ticket processing to slow down. In order to help managers implement remedial actions like workload balance or personnel adjustments, this study uses diagnostic analytics in the Work-Wise system to identify 
bottlenecks in areas where performance difficulties, delays, or inefficiencies occur.

REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
This part of the paper is a review of existing literature on ITSM, predictive analytics, machine learning, and decision-support systems, emphasizing the role of data-driven and intelligent systems in forecasting workloads, identifying performance issues, and improving operational efficiency for the system.
Local Literature
The literature indicates that service-oriented organizations, particularly in IT-enabled industries, operate within performance-driven environments that rely heavily on structured metrics and service agreements to manage efficiency and quality. These settings generate extensive operational data that can be leveraged to support forecasting, performance analysis, and informed decision-making. As a result, the integration of predictive analytics, machine learning, and decision-support systems is increasingly viewed as essential for effective ITSM and continuous service improvement.
Ellspermann, R. (2026) Call Centers Philippines: The Ultimate Outsourcing Guide 2026
Recent industry-oriented literature emphasizes the Philippine contact center industry as a mature and strategically important stage for IT-enabled service utilizations. According to the PITON-Global’s (2026) guide, the Philippines still remains to be one of the best locations for call centers and BPOs globally because of the vast pool of talent, availability of high-level talents, good 
English speaking skills, cost effectiveness and digital infrastructure. The market includes high-volume front-office and back-office jobs that must meet strict performance standards (e.g., SLAs, customer satisfaction, and agent efficiency). Call centers with certain operational qualities can benefit from predictive analytics, ML, and decision support systems due to their huge historical and real-time data.
Nuada, D. E., Mallari, M. (2025) Machine Learning–Driven Framework for Sustainable Development Assessment in the Philippines
In the Philippines, Nuada and Mallari (2025) developed a ML framework for studying sustainable development through the analysis of social, economic, and environmental data. They used algorithms like Random Forest and Gradient Boosting to identify patterns and make predictions about important variables such as GDP, unemployment, and CO₂ emissions. Their research demonstrated that machine learning can handle complex data and extract useful insights for decision-making. While their study focused on sustainability, the same approach may be used in ITSM, where machine learning can analyze KPIs and SLA data to forecast operating levels and provide recommendations about staffing or workload decisions. This demonstrates that ML is a viable approach for developing DSS in the Philippine environment.
Magellan Solutions (2025) The Importance Of KPI & SLA For BPO Philippines
	In the Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) market, KPIs and SLAs are important to guarantee the operational success and client satisfaction. KPIs are measurable measures that help organizations evaluate performance and find areas for development, such as productivity and service quality, whereas SLAs are written agreements between the providers and clients that establish the expected level of service towards the caller. By understanding and successfully implementing the use of KPIs and SLAs, it supports BPO organizations in maintaining clear expectations, driving company growth, and improving service delivery outcomes, as KPIs guide performance improvements and SLAs ensure that agreed-upon service standards are maintained. The combination of performance measurement and service commitment promotes more responsibility, better resource management, and long-term success for BPO providers and their clients.
Superstaff Outsourcing (2023) Top 5 Outsourcing Best Practices: Get the Best Out of BPOs in the Philippines
Outsourcing best practices assist businesses in forming effective collaborations with other service providers by setting clear goals, planning thorough projects, and selecting the ideal vendors to meet their needs. Thorough preparation is an essential component of efficient outsourcing, which includes identifying business issues, developing a complete project strategy, and exploring possible partners to guarantee they can satisfy specific needs and quality standards. Understanding the tasks that will be outsourced, such as customer support, technical help desk, multilingual services, and back-office functions, can also help to improve service delivery, client satisfaction, and operational efficiency. Organizations that follow these best practices can reduce risks, maintain consistent service quality, and develop stronger long-term relationships with their outsourcing partners.
Asiatel Outsourcing (n.d.) Relevance of KPI and SLA for Philippine BPO
In the Philippine BPO business, KPIs and SLAs are critical metrics for evaluating and managing service performance, operational efficiency, and client expectations.  In this context, KPIs are measurable metrics used to evaluate individual, team and organizational performance against defined objectives such as customer satisfaction, response times, and resolution rates, whereas SLAs are the official document service commitments made between providers and clients, including performance targets and accountability processes. This dual framework not only directs daily operations, but also allows for constant improvement by identifying performance gaps and recommending corrective actions. The systematic use of KPIs  and SLAs generates an environment filled with data that enables real-time decision-making and performance forecasting, which are critical for implementing complex ITSM strategies and predictive analytics technologies such as machine learning and decision-support systems. As a result, the operational emphasis on performance assessment and service quality in Philippine outsourcing provides a practical component to the theoretical constructs investigated in this work.
Foreign Literature
Foreign literature highlights the use of machine learning, predictive analytics, and decision-support systems to improve ITSM service operations. These works focus on forecasting workloads, monitoring KPIs, and automating recommendations.
Kilimci, Z. H., Akyuz, A. O., Uysal, M., Akyokus, S., Uysal, M. O., & Bulbul, B. A. (2019). An improved demand forecasting model using deep learning approach and proposed decision integration strategy for supply chain.
Kilimci et al. The study demonstrated that integrating these models with a decision-support method greatly increases forecast accuracy in supply chain operations This approach is particularly applicable to ITSM as it is accurate workload forecasting allows managers to anticipate the ticket volume increases, assign resources proactively, and avoid SLA breaches. The findings in this study supports the development of the Work-Wise system’s forecasting module, which aims to visualize trends and create staffing recommendations for IT support environments.
Ali, R., Hussain, A., Nazir, S., Khan, S., & Khan, H. U. (2023). Intelligent decision support systems—An analysis of machine learning and multicriteria decision‑making methods.
With the objective to create intelligent decision-support systems (DSS), Ali et al. (2023) addressed the integration of machine learning algorithms with multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) methodologies. The goal of their study emphasizes how these systems transform raw operational data into actionable insights by sorting priorities and visualizing KPIs. This capacity enables managers to identify difficulties, efficiently allocate resources, and improve service quality. The Work-Wise system, which computes SLA compliance, 
median and FCR rates and presents these metrics through a dashboard for well-informed decision-making, directly benefits from the findings. By integrating ML and MCDM, Work-Wise can provide structured recommendations to improve IT service operations and support proactive management initiatives.
Vitui, A., & Chen, T.‑H. (2021). MLASP: Machine learning assisted capacity planning—An industrial experience report.
MLASP, a machine learning-assisted approach for capacity planning in industrial settings, was introduced by Vitui and Chen (2021). In their approach, they predict system throughput with approximately 1% error, despite being trained on restricted data subsets, allowing for precise resource allocation and staffing decisions. This study shows that predictive analytics can translate into actionable recommendations, such as adjusting team sizes or allocating workloads to meet demand. These results verify the integration of machine learning models with decision-support dashboards to produce workload balance strategies and staffing reminders for the Work-Wise system. By following similar concepts, Work-Wise can assist IT managers in maintaining SLA compliance and optimizing operational efficiency through proactive capacity planning.
Galanti, R., de Leoni, M., Monaro, M., Navarin, N., Marazzi, A., Di Stasi, B., & Maldera, S. (2023). An explainable decision support system for predictive process analytics.
Galanti et al. (2023) introduced an explainable decision support system (DSS) that utilizes predictive process analytics and Shapley-value explanations to increase transparency and user trust. Their study showed that providing interpretable insights alongside prediction  considerably increases the acceptance of automated suggestions in operational situations. This is particularly important to the Work-Wise system, which seeks to anticipate ticket volumes, identify inefficiencies, and provide practical recommendations through a web dashboard. By integrating explicable features, Work-Wise can make certain that managers can understand the factors influencing predictions, such as the SLA status or ticket age, therefore encouraging proactive ITSM and boosting trust in decisions driven by data.
Samer Alsamadi. (2025). Machine learning-based decision support system for the integrated staffing and scheduling of call centers under uncertainties
A case study by Alsamadi (2024) on a relay call center for the deaf community developed a machine-learning based system that forecasts call volumes, estimate required staff hours, and work shifts of the agents given the operational uncertainties (e.g. human resources and budget). Through feature engineering, it enables the management for dynamic staffing adjustment through the  predictive outputs of the DSS. The development of this DSS was made for a call center namely ANGUS, wherein the management’s feedback was positive indicating that the forecasts were aligned with actual call trends allowing diagnostic and prescriptive analytics was able to identify the optimum number of agents in each shift of the day. This case study leverages ML and predictive analytics, confirms enhancement in operational efficiency and provides actionable insight on proactive staffing optimization which supports the feasibility of ITSM platforms like Work-Wise.
Cater-Steel, A., Valverde, R., Shrestha, A., & Toleman, M. (2019) Decision Support Systems for IT Service Management
	Recent DSS literature highlights the importance of data-driven decision making in the modern IT service environments. According to Cater-Steel et al. (2019), DSS frameworks help managers make better decisions by combining analytical models, operational metrics, and data structure. DSS implementations help organizations by optimizing ITSM processes, and providing structured guidance for semi-structured decisions. This proves the importance of DSS foundations in Work-Wise, which uses integrated analytics, KPIs, and forecasting guide managerial decisions.
CIO Influence. (2025). Predictive Analytics in ITSM: Reducing Downtime and Improving Incident Resolution
	Recent predictive analytics literature highlights the increase of ITSM organizations on data-driven forecasting to anticipate workload fluctuations, prevent downtime, and allocate resources more efficiently. According to CIO Influence (2025), predictive models help service teams detect early signs of service degradation, improve incident response times, and automate capacity planning by analyzing historical and real-time operational patterns. These predictive capabilities improve operational resilience by enabling the IT managers to anticipate bottlenecks before they worsen. This theoretical foundation aligns with Work-Wise, which uses predictive modeling to forecast ticket volume trends, support staffing decisions, and reduce SLA risks through proactive analytics.
Guidotti, R., Monreale, A., Ruggieri, S., Turini, F., Pedreschi, D., & Giannotti, F. (2021). A Survey of Methods for Explaining Black Box Models 
Recent literature on explainable artificial intelligence emphasizes the importance of transparent, interpretable mechanisms that show machine learning
models generate predictions. Guidotti et al. (2021) present a  comprehensive survey of major XAI approaches, such as model-agnostic methods, local explanation techniques, post-hoc interpretability tools, and transparent models. The framework categorizes explanation methods based on the type of black-box model, intended audience, and nature of explanations needed. It emphasizes the importance of clarity when deploying predictive systems in operational environments. This work aligns with the Work-Wise system's requirement for interpretable analytics, allowing managers to understand why tickets are flagged, performance inefficiencies are identified, and factors influencing the system's recommendations.

Machine Learning Model Design
Workload Balancer
	Dataset. The dataset would consist of total active tickets per agent, SLA compliance, and resolutions times collected from the different assignment groups. 
	Machine Learning Model. A Decision Tree would be applied to create rule-based thresholds that classifies workload distribution as balanced or imbalanced and 
if the current workload compromises SLA compliance.
	Feature Engineering. Ticket counts are to be made into two complementary predictors. Workload ratio is calculated by the division of an agent’s total active tickets against the team’s average tickets being handled. This would be done to normalize workload distribution, highlighting agents who carry more or less tickets than the group average. Furthermore, an extreme ratio would be calculated by comparing the busiest agent’s total active ticket against the freest agent, capturing the spread between the extremes revealing severe overload situations. SLA compliance would be then used as a quality factor, each agent’s compliance is compared against the SLA threshold whether the current workload distribution affects ITSM Support Employee performance. By combining workload ratios of average and extreme with SLA compliance, the model would detect workload imbalance resulting in quality-based breaches. Through these two features robustness of the Decision Tree would be ensured, catching subtle distribution issues as well as severe cases of overload that compromise SLA compliance. 
	Training / Test Split. The dataset would be split 70/30 into training and test sets accordingly. Stratified sampling would be used to ensure balanced representation of both balanced and imbalanced workloads in the training and test sets.
	Evaluation Metrics. Model performance would be measured by precision, recall, F1 score, and accuracy to validate the system’s capability of identifying imbalance and to minimize misclassification.
Non-FCR Detector
	Dataset. The dataset for the Non-FCR Detector are ticket category, assigned employee, and FCR status. These statistics would serve as the derivations to calculate FCR rates per category and agent FCR rate
	Machine Learning Model. Logistic Regression would be applied for its ability to predict Non-FCR event probability. The model would take data from both category-level and employee-level categories to find recurring patterns of Non-FCR events
	Feature Engineering. Non-FCR cases would be flagged by analyzing FCR outcomes then analyzed by the category and employee level in tandem. This pairing distinguishes the root cause of Non-FCR cases whether this would be a systemic issue with ITSM Support Employees struggling with a ticket category or employee-specific issues where only one employee struggles with a specific ticket category.
	Training / Test Split. The dataset would be split 70/30 into training and test sets accordingly with stratified sampling to ensure balance of FCR and Non-FCR cases in both sets. This ensures model training and evaluation is fair for both outcomes.
	Evaluation Metrics. Model performance would be measured by precision, recall, F1 score, and ROC-AUC. The mentioned metrics validates the model’s ability to identify recurring Non-FCR cases and balance false alarms against missed detections.  
Staffing Intelligence
	Dataset. The dataset would include historical ticket trends, staffing levels, and SLA compliance which provide the baseline to derive staffing recommendations.
	Machine Learning Model. Time-series forecasting (Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (SARIMA) or Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA)) would be applied to predict future ticket volumes partnered with a Regression model to estimate the staff capacity required to satisfy the threshold SLA compliance. The predicted ticket volume against staffing capacity would enable the system to provide staffing insight
	Feature Engineering. The predicted ticket volume would be derived into seasonal ticket demand trends capturing workload fluctuations. Staffing capacity is then converted into agent to ticket ratios projecting the expected workload ITSM Support Employee. SLA compliance rates would then validate if quality of service is satisfied 
allowing the system to assess if the current workforce is sufficient to maintain the threshold SLA compliance.
	Training / Test Split. Historical ticket data is split 70/30 into training and test sets to ensure seasonal patterns and compliance variations are captured for fair evaluations.
	Evaluation Metrics. The model would be measured by Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE). Workforce sufficiency predictions are to be validated through precision, recall, and F1 to ensure the system identifies periods of high ticket volume where staffing is unable to meet the threshold SLA compliance.
